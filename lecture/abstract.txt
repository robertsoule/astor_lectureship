Rethinking Distributed Systems for Efficiency

Much of distributed systems research over the past few decades has focused on questions of scale, i.e., how we can do more with more. Often lost in this focus on scale, however, has been an examination of efficiency, i.e., how to do more with less.  Focusing on efficiency is important because it reduces operational bloat, decreases financial pressures, and can result in sustainable designs that diminish environmental impact. 

In this talk, I present three systems that are designed to make distributed systems more efficient. The first step in improving efficiency is measurement. I discuss a novel approach to telemetry that embeds an SQL query engine directly into an operating system kernel. With a system for better measurement in place, I present a system to improve the efficiency of network I/O. Accio is a novel software/hardware co-design that improves performance while maintaining POSIX-standard APIs. Finally, I discuss three key observations on how synchronous consensus protocols, which offer clear advantages over other protocols in terms of resource consumption, can be made practical for public cloud environments.
